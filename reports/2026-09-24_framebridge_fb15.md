# FrameBridge FB15 — a second physical cue for winding magnitude

Status: **fixed-rule CPU research result; promising review/QA improvement, not production export or a demonstrated fitter gain**. This report follows the [method and population freeze](../docs/49_fb15_surface_crest_protocol.md) and does not revise the crest rule after seeing validation. The strongest test here is collection-disjoint from the 48-pair method-selection pilot, but it is still drawn from the already-studied FB08 development source. The FB14 and FB08 holdout replays are explicitly retrospective.

## Why this matters

FB08 learned when an E1 chord is geometrically applicable; FB14 showed that even high-confidence, normal-aligned accepted chords can have the wrong integer wrap magnitude. FB15 asks a narrower question: can a differently sampled public surface field provide an *independent disagreement signal* before an E1 magnitude reaches the fitter? A simple seven-ray crest count, chosen on an enriched 48-pair pilot and frozen before the next SDT payload was acquired, gives a material gain on the selected Paris 4 development population. It does not repair E1 signs or eliminate residual errors.

## Data, method, and selection

The public `las_008_surf_sdt.ome.zarr.respool_g1` metadata and sparse pool index are pinned by SHA-256. The field shape is `[9473,4087,4087]`; the working-to-field coordinate scale is 2. For each pair, sample seven parallel rays at one working-voxel spacing, smooth each uint8 trace with `[0.25,0.5,0.25]`, count crests with height ≥130, prominence ≥2, and separation ≥6 samples, then round the median count. All seven rays must be valid; zero is a no-agreement/review cue. The cue supplies **absolute magnitude only**. A signed SDT prediction borrows E1's sign. This is an image-feature hypothesis, **not proof that each crest is a physical sheet crossing**.

The pilot selected eight examples from each true magnitude 1/2/3 × E1 correct/wrong stratum (48 total). Its 40/48 SDT magnitude score versus 24/48 E1 was **selection-biased method development**; two stricter crest variants scored 36/48 and 29/48. The fixed v1 source hash and validation candidate hash were recorded locally before validation SDT bytes were obtained. This is a local freeze, not an externally timestamped preregistration.

The primary arm hash-ranked 300 FB08 relative-development IDs, excluding **all 41 collections represented in the 48-pair pilot**, yielding 120 other collections. It was selected without reading those rows' E1/SDT outcomes, but the FB08 development source itself had already been used in prior research. The 90 FB14 absolute-anchor pairs, only two dependent collections with outcomes already known, are a cross-source stress arm. The original 207 FB08 holdout-gate emits were scored afterward as a retrospective QA check; their 17 errors were also known before FB15.

The holdout row is **conditioned on the original FB08 gate**, so its SDT full score is not comparable with a random 207-pair source and no population score is inferred from it. In that row all 17 wrong signed E1 proposals were magnitude errors. The SDT cue alone is actually less accurate than E1 within that high-confidence gate (174 versus 190 exact); its value there is selective disagreement. Seven of the 17 wrong gate emits still agree with SDT and remain unsafe.

The unchanged FB08 gate on the 300-pair primary arm had **72/74** exact signed relations. Requiring fixed SDT magnitude agreement retained **64/64**, flagging both wrong and reviewing eight correct; E1-confidence ranking *within that same gate* at 64 retains **62/64**. On the previously inspected FB14 arm, the gate had **9/13** exact; agreement retained **7/7**, flagging all four wrong and reviewing two correct; confidence top seven *within the gate* retained **5/7**. On the retrospective original FB08 holdout gate, SDT agreement retained **166/173** versus **156/173** for within-gate E1 confidence ranking at the same count. These are useful fixed-coverage comparisons, but the small gated subsets and source dependence forbid a universal precision claim.

The primary-arm SDT magnitude gains concentrate in harder classes: for true magnitude 1, E1 152/168 versus SDT 150/168; magnitude 2, 65/104 versus 92/104; magnitude 3, 12/28 versus 25/28. Thus it is not merely a better detector of easy adjacent wraps. The class-1 regression matters, as do the four residual primary-arm E1 errors among 209 agreement cases. The FB14 arm includes four true-magnitude-4 pairs and only two SDT magnitudes correct; it is too small for a class-4 reliability claim. The retrospective holdout gate retains 7/17 wrong signed relations despite agreement.

Pairs within point collections are dependent. A 5,000-replicate **collection-cluster** bootstrap over the 120 primary-arm collections gave a descriptive SDT-minus-E1 signed-accuracy difference of **+12.33 percentage points**, percentile 95% interval **+5.59 to +19.09**. The E1-wrong-magnitude flag rate was 94.37% (88.37–98.75), correct-magnitude review rate 10.48% (6.31–15.07), agreement coverage 69.67% (63.85–75.52), and E1 signed exact among agreements 98.09% (96.08–99.56). These intervals describe variation within this selected source, **not** transfer to another scroll, scan, or automatically generated candidate distribution. Do not compute pairwise binomial intervals as if pairs were independent.

## Failure taxonomy and release decision

The cue catches E1 one-wrap undercounts that were invisible to confidence and normal alignment, particularly in true 2/3 cases. It also finds some *overcounts* in the original holdout gate. It cannot certify every accepted relation: 7/17 original holdout gate errors remain after magnitude agreement. Twenty-four of 190 correct holdout-gate proposals are withheld, a real coverage cost. Two zero-crest primary pairs and two zero-crest retrospective holdout pairs are reviewed, never promoted to zero-winding truth. Ray counts are not unanimous on all pairs, and their dispersion should be exposed as diagnostic information rather than hidden behind the median.

Two compact counterexamples make the distinction concrete. On the already-seen FB14 stress pair `FB14:1:49:53`, human signed difference is −4, E1 gives −3 at confidence 0.854, and all seven SDT rays count four crests: the adapter would request **magnitude-disagreement review**. On original FB08 holdout-gate pair `relative/244/2560/2562`, truth is +2, E1 gives +3 at confidence 0.883, and all seven SDT rays also count three: **agreement still leaves a wrong edge**. These IDs and counts come from the ignored scored artifacts; neither example proves crest-to-sheet semantics. The second is why even unanimous agreement is not production certification.

The field's physical semantics, CT-to-field registration, independent dense-source parity, and official-fitter benefit are still unverified. FB13 established same-byte sparse/dense SciPy interpolation parity only. The opt-in `src/scroll_lab/sdt_review.py` adapter exposes agreement, disagreement, zero/missing rays, and ray-count spread as reason-coded **research review** evidence. It never returns production export permission, even when the two magnitudes agree. The `FRAME_REGISTRATION_UNVERIFIED` latch remains closed; **no numeric relation was exported into production fitting**. This result can be presented confidently as a reproducible two-cue trust/review improvement with a striking magnitude-2/3 signal, not as an already won spiral-fitting benchmark.

The next consequential test is an untouched, independently verified positive-winding source with enough collections and hard magnitudes, followed by a fixed-common-input fitter comparison. The review decision must be evaluated at matched coverage against confidence and current gate, and residual seven holdout errors merit a counterexample gallery. If the only available test reuses these annotations, label it retrospective. A GPU/Colab run is **not** needed for this SDT cue; it becomes justified for an official fitter treatment/control only after input and outcome contracts are fixed.
