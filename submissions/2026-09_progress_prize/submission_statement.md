# FrameBridge: auditable winding-evidence QA for virtual unwrapping

FrameBridge is an open-source validation layer for winding evidence used in virtual unwrapping. It makes coordinate frames, sparse field support, and geometric applicability explicit, and records reason-coded decisions before a winding proposal is trusted by downstream tools. The goal is to catch cases where a plausible-looking constraint is unsupported by the sampled field or is being applied outside the geometry where its assumptions hold.

On the frozen Paris 4 FB08 evaluation, the unchanged normal-alignment and confidence gate retained 190 of 207 held-out relative human-annotation proposals exactly (91.79%) at 19.73% candidate coverage. On the separately selected, spatially buffered same-winding population it emitted 0 false nonzero proposals among 1,742 candidates, compared with 397 for confidence-only E1. In the matched geometry window it retained 123/130 relative proposals exactly and emitted 0/646 false nonzero same-winding proposals; confidence-only retained 143/151 and emitted 150/646. This is a conditional applicability and false-accept tradeoff with lower coverage—not a fitter or geometry improvement. Seventeen accepted relative proposals had the wrong wrap magnitude.

A separate FB09 test on 400 traced-fiber pairs showed 0 nonzero proposals from the gate versus 84 from confidence-thresholded E1. Its zero labels are constructed from within-trace continuity, not human-verified pairwise winding labels. FB15 adds a model-derived magnitude-disagreement review signal, but seven of 17 known retrospective holdout errors remained unflagged and its SDT crests are not independent observations of physical crossings.

We also report failure cases: the FB14 gate did not beat equal-coverage confidence ranking on two dependent absolute-annotation collections; simple graph downweighting did not improve exact assignments in FB16; and the official-fitter FB25 seed-17 comparison is negative (0.57394 for FrameBridge versus 0.59654 for patch-only). FB25 remains incomplete at 2/8 outcomes, so no fitter gain is claimed.

The CPU demo fetches hash-pinned public metadata, checks the sparse index/table inverse, and plans byte ranges for one mesh without downloading CT payloads. It reproduces 10,683 occupied bricks and 717 ranges. Full scientific replay needs separately acquired public challenge inputs; none are bundled. Physical registration, blind review, and production export remain unverified. We offer FrameBridge as an auditable evidence-QA tool with a documented coverage tradeoff and explicit negative results, not as a winning unwrapping pipeline.
